# Federal hemp definition change: November 12 and December 11, 2026

**Last verified: 2026-11-02.** Check the sources again before relying on this after that date.

This is not legal advice. Whether a specific product may be sold, shipped or card-processed is a decision for the merchant's own counsel. Rupert records and enforces the merchant's approved catalog (roadmap F69 and F70). It does not decide product legality.

## Summary

- **New hemp definition.** Section 781 of division B of [Public Law 119-37](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ37/PLAW-119publ37.pdf) (H.R. 5371, signed November 12, 2025) rewrites the federal definition of hemp (7 U.S.C. 1639o). It takes effect 365 days after enactment, originally **November 12, 2026**.
  - **Total THC:** the plant limit becomes 0.3% *total* THC including THCA, not delta-9 THC alone.
  - **Per-container cap:** final hemp-derived cannabinoid products are excluded if they contain more than **0.4 mg combined total THC (including THCA) and similar-effect cannabinoids per container**.
  - **Synthesized cannabinoids:** cannabinoids that the plant cannot naturally produce are excluded. So are cannabinoids synthesized or manufactured outside the plant.
- **Most of it is delayed to December 11, 2026.** Section 2019 of [Public Law 119-103](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ103/PLAW-119publ103.pdf) (H.R. 6500, Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, signed September 2, 2026) applies §781 until December 11, 2026 only to products containing **cannabinoids that are not capable of being naturally produced by the plant** (paragraphs (1)(C)(ii)(I) and (1)(C)(iv)(I)).
- **What applies when:**
  - **From November 12, 2026:** the exclusion of non-naturally-producible cannabinoids.
  - **From December 11, 2026:** everything else, including total THC (THCA) at the plant level, the 0.4 mg per-container cap, and plant-natural cannabinoids synthesized outside the plant (for example, converted delta-8 or delta-9).
- **Another extension is uncertain.**
  - December 11 is also when the stopgap funding expires.
  - The House-passed FY2027 Agriculture appropriations bill (H.R. 8646) contains no further delay (CRS IF13136, September 28, 2026 update).
  - Foley Hoag reports that a White House official committed to no extensions beyond December 11, and that the official was later reported to be leaving.
  - Hemp Law Group reports the White House told Senate Republicans the 30-day delay would be the last it supports.
  - Bills that would delay further (H.R. 7024, H.R. 7010, S. 3686) or repeal the change (H.R. 6209) have been introduced, not enacted.
- **Payments.**
  - Visa and Mastercard prohibit marijuana. A product outside the federal hemp definition is treated as marijuana federally.
  - Acquirers and processors therefore approve a merchant's **exact catalog**, and are expected to drop non-qualifying products.
  - Mainstream platforms have already left the category:
    - **Shopify Payments** does not support hemp, CBD or THC products. It points sellers to compatible third-party providers such as DigiPay and Bankful.
    - **Square** told sellers on August 7, 2026 to remove CBD and hemp items by October 15, 2026, and its CBD program page now returns "not found".
    - **Stripe** prohibits CBD over the legal THC limit and CBD edibles, and reviews negligible-THC CBD case by case.
    - **PayPal** prohibits the category.
  - Mislabeling products or merchant category codes to get approved risks termination and a MATCH listing, which ends all card acceptance.

## What it likely means for a hemp retailer (confirm with counsel)

| Product type | Likely status after December 11, 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| THCA flower, pre-rolls, concentrates, THCA vapes | Fail the total-THC test; no longer federal hemp |
| Delta-9 or delta-8 edibles, drinks or tinctures over 0.4 mg THC per container | Excluded by the per-container cap |
| Converted or synthesized cannabinoids (for example delta-8 made from CBD, HHC, THC-O) | Non-naturally-producible ones from **November 12**; plant-natural but synthesized ones from December 11 |
| CBD isolate and broad-spectrum (non-detectable THC), pet CBD | Likely still hemp if each container stays at or under 0.4 mg total THC; requires a COA per batch |
| Full-spectrum CBD | At risk: trace THC can exceed 0.4 mg per container |
| Non-cannabinoid goods (mushrooms, melatonin, accessories) | Not affected by this definition |

State law applies separately and can be stricter, with different dates.

## Customer summary (ready to send)

> Federal law (Public Law 119-37, §781) changes the definition of hemp. Hemp will be measured by total THC, including THCA. Finished products will be limited to 0.4 mg of total THC per container. The September 2 stopgap funding law (Public Law 119-103) moved most of these rules to **December 11, 2026**. However, products containing cannabinoids the plant cannot naturally produce lose hemp status on **November 12, 2026**. After December 11, THCA flower and concentrates, and THC edibles or drinks above 0.4 mg per container, will most likely no longer qualify as hemp. Card processors only approve products that do. Shopify Payments, Square, Stripe and PayPal already decline this category, so card acceptance depends on a hemp-approved processor and an approved product list. Congress may or may not extend the deadline again; plan for December 11. Please confirm your own product list with your attorney. This summary is not legal advice.
